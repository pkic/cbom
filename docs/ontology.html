CONCEPT

Ontology: node, edge, asset

Three words carry the whole methodology. Keeping them straight is what lets a profile talk about connections, not just algorithms.

The three terms

TermDefinitionIn the example
Cryptographic relationship (edge / interface)The locus where cryptography is applied between parties. Not itself a cryptographic asset.The TLS 1.3 session; the SSH session.
Endpoint (node)A party to a relationship.client (browser / admin) and server (nginx / host).
Cryptographic assetAn algorithm, protocol, certificate, or key the relationship uses.X25519, AES-256-GCM, the RSA certificate, OpenSSL.

Why the distinction matters

PQC migration analysis is about connections, not just inventory. "We use ML-KEM somewhere" is far less useful than "this specific connection between service A and service B still negotiates a classical group." The first is a node fact; the second is an edge fact. Conflating them breaks the analysis — which is why the working group settled on cryptographic relationship (edge) paired with endpoint (node) as the core terms, and why interfaceType and endpoint roles are attributes of the relationship, not of any single algorithm.

Neither CycloneDX nor SPDX has a first-class object that is the relationship today. Both approximate it by attaching edge attributes to a protocol asset. Closing that gap is the working group's first-class attributed-edge model — see Challenges and Formats.
