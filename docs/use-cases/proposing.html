WRITING ONE

Proposing a use case

How a use case enters this section, how it is developed into a worked example, and what each status means. Read this before copying the template.

When a use case is worth proposing

A use case is worth proposing when a consumer and a decision can be named, and the facts that decision needs are not already required by the interface disclosure baseline. It is not a sector, a product or a field a format happens to offer. Starting from a format's fields produces a profile with no stated purpose and no way to argue about whether it asks for the right things.

  1. Open a topic on the aspect issues stating the consumer, the actions being chosen between, and the questions that decision raises.
  2. Add it to the register on the overview and to its group page as proposed. If it fits neither group, raise that first: it may be a third kind of profile, or not a use case at all.
  3. Copy the template and work down it. A page that is only half filled in is still worth a pull request: the blank sections are the ones the working group has to answer.

A use case that turns out to be served by an existing profile, by a depth of an existing family, or by the change core alone is a better outcome than a new profile, and should be argued for on that basis.

Status

StatusWhat it requires
ProposedA consumer and a decision are named. Nothing more. It is an invitation to develop the use case, not a commitment that it needs a profile.
DescribedThe consumer, the decision, the constraint the producer is under and what the profile would need are written on the group page.
Draft worked exampleA page in the eight-part shape below exists. Rules, documents or verdicts are not yet settled, and the page says which.
Worked exampleA rules file that passes requirements C1 to C17 of Conformance, at least one document that conforms to it and one that does not, and verdicts checked with the validator.

The shape of a worked example

Every worked example follows the same eight parts, in the same order, because each part is the input to the next. The shape is the twelve steps of Method grouped for reading rather than for working. The template is the same shape as a blank.

  1. The decision being supported. Who the consumer is, what actions it is choosing between, and the questions it has to answer to choose. Stating the actions is what makes the rest checkable: a decision answerable in only one way is not a decision, and a profile written from one has no principled place to stop.
  2. The constraint the producer is under. What the party filling the document in can actually observe. An operator of its own service can be asked for more than a vendor shipping a product it cannot inspect, and a profile that ignores the difference is unsatisfiable rather than demanding.
  3. From questions to attributes. Each question turned into one fact that can be declared at the boundary. The questions that do not survive this step matter as much as the ones that do, and are recorded rather than dropped.
  4. The profile definition. The product-level, per-interface and group rules, with their conformance levels; any rule inherited from a base profile that this one tightens; and any rule that applies only under a stated condition.
  5. A worked interface. One real interface with every required attribute filled in, so the rules can be read against something concrete rather than in the abstract.
  6. What the profile deliberately excludes. Each exclusion with its reason. Without this the profile cannot be reviewed: a reader cannot tell an exclusion that was argued from an attribute nobody thought of.
  7. Expressing it in a CBOM. How the attributes land in CycloneDX and SPDX, and which of them the formats have no native field for.
  8. Recorded decisions and open questions. What was settled while writing it, and what the working group has not settled.

Why worked examples are kept out of the methodology

  • Instances multiply and a specification should not. If each worked example were part of the methodology, the length of the deliverable would depend on how many use cases exist.
  • They have different readers. Someone assessing the methodology needs one example. Someone with a migration to plan needs theirs in full.
  • They are governed differently. A profile is versioned and stewarded on its own schedule, as Governance sets out.
